\section{Introduction}

The Music Player is a headless app inside \texttt{voxd} that projects the
daemon's one active source onto a lux scene and translates in-scene clicks back
into playback commands. It adds no playback machinery: playing a saved album is
\texttt{ProgramService.replay\_album}, stopping is \texttt{off}, and advancing
within the album is the playback loop driving \texttt{SelectionPlayback.rotate}.
Those transitions --- \texttt{StartRadio}, \texttt{RadioOff},
\texttt{RadioRotate}, and the \texttt{MusicRemove} refusal of a playing album ---
are already modelled and \texttt{fuzz}-clean in \texttt{docs/audio-programs.tex}.

This specification pins the one new, stable object: the \emph{PlayerView}, and
the three invariants the design must preserve across every player transition.
The phase-2 lux connection/subscribe/lease lifecycle is modelled in
Section~\ref{sec:conn}, now that lux has pinned its subscribe and lease API.

\section{Basic Types and Modes}

An \texttt{ALBUM} is an opaque catalog identifier --- the id \texttt{vox music
list} prints and the scene's Play button carries. It is a catalog key, never a
filesystem path; the model treats it as opaque, exactly as
\texttt{audio-programs.tex} does.

\begin{zed}
  [ALBUM]
\end{zed}

The player is a two-mode machine: nothing playing, or one album playing.

\begin{zed}
  PlayerMode ::= idle | playing
\end{zed}

\section{State}
\label{sec:state}

The \texttt{PlayerView} carries the catalog of saved albums, the mode, the
optional playing album (a set of at most one, mirroring the ``optional Part''
cursor of \texttt{audio-programs.tex}), and the now-playing cursor
\texttt{(index, of)} --- the 1-based track position and the album's track count,
both zero when idle.

The predicate carries every invariant. \texttt{I1} bounds the playing album to
at most one. \texttt{I2} is the biconditional the design names ``now-playing
present iff playing'': the mode is \texttt{playing} exactly when one album is
playing, exactly when the cursor is live (\texttt{of} $\geq 1$). \texttt{I3}
contains the playing album in the catalog. The cursor-validity conjuncts tie the
index into $1 \upto of$ while playing and pin it to zero while idle.

\begin{schema}{PlayerView}
  catalogued : \power ALBUM \\
  mode : PlayerMode \\
  album : \power ALBUM \\
  index : \nat \\
  of : \nat
  \where
  \# album \leq 1 \\
  album \subseteq catalogued \\
  (mode = playing \iff \# album = 1) \\
  (mode = idle \iff album = \emptyset) \\
  (mode = playing \implies (1 \leq index \land index \leq of \land of \geq 1)) \\
  (mode = idle \implies (index = 0 \land of = 0))
\end{schema}

The now-playing cursor is the \texttt{(index, of)} pair the status API reports as
``Part $N$ of $M$''; \texttt{I2} makes ``present iff playing'' a fact of the state
schema, not a runtime check. Because \texttt{PlayerMode} has only two
inhabitants, $mode = idle$ and $mode = playing$ are complementary, so the two
biconditionals are consistent: $album = \emptyset \iff \# album = 0 \iff mode
\neq playing$.

\section{Initialisation}

The daemon starts with a (possibly empty) catalog and nothing playing.

\begin{schema}{PlayerInit}
  PlayerView' \\
  cat0? : \power ALBUM
  \where
  catalogued' = cat0? \\
  mode' = idle \\
  album' = \emptyset \\
  index' = 0 \\
  of' = 0
\end{schema}

\section{Player Transitions}

Each transition is the projection of an \texttt{audio-programs.tex} operation
onto the view. They are gated on mode alone, never on a session --- any client
(the lux button, the CLI, or an MCP tool) may drive any of them, which is why the
scene must re-push on every state change regardless of origin.

\subsection{PlayerPlay --- play a saved album}

A client plays the album \texttt{target?}, whose ready-track count is
\texttt{count?}. The album must be catalogued and non-empty ($count? \geq 1$):
\texttt{replay\_album} refuses an unknown id and an album with no playable
tracks. There is \emph{no} mode precondition: from \texttt{idle} this is
\texttt{StartRadio}; while already \texttt{playing} it is
\texttt{SwitchSelection}, displacing the old album. Either way the after-state
has exactly one album playing, so \texttt{I1} holds and two albums never play at
once.

\begin{schema}{PlayerPlay}
  \Delta PlayerView \\
  target? : ALBUM \\
  count? : \nat
  \where
  target? \in catalogued \\
  count? \geq 1 \\
  catalogued' = catalogued \\
  mode' = playing \\
  album' = \{ target? \} \\
  index' = 1 \\
  of' = count?
\end{schema}

The cursor starts at track~1 of \texttt{count?}. Because $count? \geq 1$, the
after-state satisfies the cursor-validity invariant ($1 \leq 1 \leq of'$ and
$of' \geq 1$), and because $target? \in catalogued$, \texttt{I3}
($album' \subseteq catalogued'$) holds. \texttt{PlayerPlay} is the single verb
for both ``start'' and ``switch'', exactly as \texttt{SwitchSelection} $\defs$
\texttt{StartRadio} in \texttt{audio-programs.tex}.

\subsection{PlayerTrackEnd --- auto-advance within the album}

When the current track ends, the playback loop advances the cursor to another
track of the same album, avoiding an immediate repeat when the album holds more
than one and replaying when it holds exactly one. This is \texttt{RadioRotate}
projected onto the cursor: the album and its count are unchanged, only the index
moves.

\begin{schema}{PlayerTrackEnd}
  \Delta PlayerView
  \where
  mode = playing \\
  catalogued' = catalogued \\
  mode' = playing \\
  album' = album \\
  of' = of \\
  1 \leq index' \land index' \leq of \\
  (of \geq 2 \implies index' \neq index) \\
  (of = 1 \implies index' = index)
\end{schema}

The automatic track-end advance and a user-driven \texttt{next}/\texttt{skip} are
one transition, mirroring \texttt{RadioNext} $\defs$ \texttt{RadioRotate}. The
mode, album, and count are all held, so \texttt{I1}, \texttt{I2}, and \texttt{I3}
are trivially preserved; only the cursor moves, and it stays in range.

\subsection{PlayerStop --- stop, return to idle}

Stopping tears the source down and returns the view to \texttt{idle}: no album,
no cursor. This is \texttt{RadioOff}. It is enabled while playing; a stop while
already idle is a no-op the daemon need not model as a transition.

\begin{schema}{PlayerStop}
  \Delta PlayerView
  \where
  mode = playing \\
  catalogued' = catalogued \\
  mode' = idle \\
  album' = \emptyset \\
  index' = 0 \\
  of' = 0
\end{schema}

The after-state is the \texttt{idle} shape: \texttt{I2} holds because emptying
the album empties the cursor, discharging ``stop returns to idle''.

\section{Catalog Transitions}

The catalog changes under the player via \texttt{music new} and \texttt{music
remove}. They are modelled here only for their interaction with the view ---
specifically, that a playing album cannot be removed.

\subsection{CatalogAdd --- a fresh album appears}

\texttt{music new} files a fresh album. It grows the catalog and leaves playback
untouched, so the scene re-pushes with a new row but the same now-playing.

\begin{schema}{CatalogAdd}
  \Delta PlayerView \\
  new? : ALBUM
  \where
  new? \notin catalogued \\
  catalogued' = catalogued \cup \{ new? \} \\
  mode' = mode \\
  album' = album \\
  index' = index \\
  of' = of
\end{schema}

Because the catalog only grows, $album \subseteq catalogued'$ still holds and
\texttt{I3} is preserved with playback unchanged.

\subsection{CatalogRemove --- delete a non-playing album}

\texttt{music remove} deletes a catalogued album, but the playing album is
refused --- the model image of the \texttt{active\_backing\_locators} guard and of
\texttt{MusicRemove}'s precondition in \texttt{audio-programs.tex}. The
precondition $gone? \notin album$ is exactly ``you cannot delete the album you
are playing''.

\begin{schema}{CatalogRemove}
  \Delta PlayerView \\
  gone? : ALBUM
  \where
  gone? \in catalogued \\
  gone? \notin album \\
  catalogued' = catalogued \setminus \{ gone? \} \\
  mode' = mode \\
  album' = album \\
  index' = index \\
  of' = of
\end{schema}

The guard is what preserves \texttt{I3} across a removal: with $gone? \notin
album$ and $album \subseteq catalogued$, removing \texttt{gone?} leaves
$album \subseteq catalogued'$. Deleting the playing album would break
$album' \subseteq catalogued'$, so the invariant is not decoration --- it forces
the guard rather than the guard being an ad-hoc check.

\section{The Connection, Subscribe, and Lease Lifecycle}
\label{sec:conn}

The earlier draft of this specification deferred the phase-2 connection model,
because lux's subscribe and lease API was not yet pinned. It is now pinned, and
this section supplies the model in full; the deferral in Section~6 of the design
is discharged here.

Phase~2 gives \texttt{voxd} a receive leg: \emph{one} outbound WebSocket to
\texttt{luxd} (the \texttt{LuxHubClient}) carrying both pub-sub subscriptions and
menu-callback invocations. The connection is a three-mode machine ---
\texttt{disconnected} $\to$ \texttt{connected} $\to$ \texttt{subscribed} --- with
automatic reconnect on a drop. An inbound \texttt{music.play}\{album\} or
\texttt{music.stop} received while subscribed is dispatched to the playback
transitions of \texttt{audio-programs.tex} (\texttt{StartRadio} for a play,
\texttt{RadioOff} for a stop); events that arrive during a connection gap are
\emph{buffered Hub-side} and \emph{drain in order} on reconnect, \emph{as long as
the reconnect precedes lease expiry} --- an expired lease sweeps the buffer, so a
click undrained by then is discarded as stale (fork~(B), settled with the lux
agent). A menu lease of $30$ seconds, carried on the identity, is renewed by any
authenticated contact and withdraws \texttt{voxd}'s menu entries only after the
lease expires with no contact --- the state that models ``\texttt{voxd} died''.

The model pins five invariants, stated in the state predicate below:

\begin{description}
  \item[(I)] at most one live connection, and at most one subscription, which
    can exist only over that one connection;
  \item[(II)] an event received while subscribed is dispatched exactly once;
  \item[(III)] events buffered during a gap drain in order on reconnect
    \emph{provided the reconnect precedes lease expiry}; a lease that expires
    first discards the buffer as stale (this is the gap-bounded reading, fork
    (B) below);
  \item[(IV)] the lease is renewed on any authenticated contact and expires only
    after the full \texttt{leaseTtl} elapses with no contact;
  \item[(V)] each \texttt{music.play}/\texttt{music.stop} event maps to exactly
    one playback transition.
\end{description}

\subsection{Types and the lease constant}

A \texttt{ZBOOL} is a Z boolean, named to avoid the ProB/B clash with
\texttt{BOOL}. It flags whether \texttt{voxd}'s menu entries are currently
present Hub-side.

\begin{zed}
  ZBOOL ::= ztrue | zfalse
\end{zed}

The connection has three modes and the lease three phases. Separating the
\emph{connection} mode (\texttt{voxd}'s view of its own socket) from the
\emph{lease} phase (the Hub's view of \texttt{voxd}'s liveness) is deliberate:
after a drop the socket is \texttt{disconnected} while the lease is still
\texttt{held} --- the grace window in which the menu stays up and clicks buffer.

\begin{zed}
  ConnMode ::= disconnected | connected | subscribed
\end{zed}

\begin{zed}
  LeasePhase ::= noLease | held | expired
\end{zed}

An inbound event is a \texttt{music.play} carrying an album, or a
\texttt{music.stop}. \texttt{ALBUM} is the catalog identifier already declared
in Section~\ref{sec:state}.

\begin{zed}
  MusicEvent ::= playEvent \ldata ALBUM \rdata | stopEvent
\end{zed}

A playback transition is one of the two \texttt{audio-programs.tex} verbs the
receive leg drives. They are named here only to record the mapping; the
transitions themselves are modelled and \texttt{fuzz}-clean there.

\begin{zed}
  PlaybackOp ::= startRadio | radioOff
\end{zed}

The dispatch function is total and deterministic: every event names exactly one
playback transition --- a play starts a radio, a stop stops it. This total
function \emph{is} invariant~(V), pinned before any state is declared.

\begin{axdef}
  dispatch : MusicEvent \fun PlaybackOp
  \where
  \forall a : ALBUM @ dispatch~(playEvent~a) = startRadio \\
  dispatch~stopEvent = radioOff
\end{axdef}

The lease runs for \texttt{leaseTtl} seconds; the design value is $30$.

\begin{axdef}
  leaseTtl : \nat_1
  \where
  leaseTtl = 30
\end{axdef}

\subsection{State}

The \texttt{LuxListener} carries the connection mode; two $0 \upto 1$ counters
\texttt{conns} and \texttt{subs} that make invariant~(I) an explicit,
model-checkable predicate rather than a mere consequence of \texttt{mode} being a
scalar; the Hub-side \texttt{buffer} of events awaiting drain; the
\texttt{dispatched} history of events handed to playback, in dispatch order; the
\texttt{fired} history of the playback transitions those dispatches drove; the
lease phase, its age in seconds, and the \texttt{present} flag for the menu
entries.

\begin{schema}{LuxListener}
  mode : ConnMode \\
  conns : 0 \upto 1 \\
  subs : 0 \upto 1 \\
  buffer : \seq MusicEvent \\
  dispatched : \seq MusicEvent \\
  fired : \seq PlaybackOp \\
  leasePhase : LeasePhase \\
  leaseAge : 0 \upto leaseTtl \\
  present : ZBOOL
  \where
  (conns = 1 \iff mode \neq disconnected) \\
  (subs = 1 \iff mode = subscribed) \\
  subs \leq conns \\
  (buffer \neq \langle \rangle \implies (mode = disconnected \land present = ztrue)) \\
  fired = dispatched \comp dispatch \\
  (leasePhase = held \implies leaseAge < leaseTtl) \\
  (leasePhase = expired \implies leaseAge = leaseTtl) \\
  (leasePhase = noLease \implies leaseAge = 0) \\
  (present = ztrue \iff leasePhase = held)
\end{schema}

The first three conjuncts are invariant~(I): \texttt{conns} and \texttt{subs} are
each at most one by type, \texttt{subs} $\leq$ \texttt{conns} says a subscription
rides on the single connection, and the two biconditionals tie both counters to
\texttt{mode}. The fourth is the state face of the gap-bounded invariant~(III): a
non-empty buffer can exist only during the \emph{grace window} --- the socket
down \emph{and} the lease still held, so the menu is still present
($present = ztrue \iff leasePhase = held$) --- and there are exactly two exits
from a buffered state, \texttt{ReconnectAndDrain} (which drains it) and
\texttt{LeaseExpire} (which discards it). A buffered click therefore survives if
and only if the reconnect wins the race against the lease clock. The fifth is
invariant~(V) lifted to
the history: \texttt{fired} is exactly \texttt{dispatched} composed with
\texttt{dispatch}, so every dispatched event contributed exactly one playback
transition, the mapped one, in order; hence $\# fired = \# dispatched$. The last
four are invariant~(IV): a \texttt{held} lease has not exceeded its age, an
\texttt{expired} lease sits exactly at \texttt{leaseTtl}, an unborn lease is at
age zero, and the menu is present exactly while the lease is held.

The exactly-once discipline of invariant~(II) is a property of the transitions
rather than of a single state: an event instance is created by exactly one
arrival --- \texttt{ReceiveEvent} appends it straight to \texttt{dispatched} while
subscribed, or \texttt{EnqueueDuringGap} appends it to \texttt{buffer} while
disconnected --- and \texttt{dispatched} and \texttt{buffer} only ever grow by a
single append or, on \texttt{ReconnectAndDrain}, move the whole buffer across in
one step. No event is ever dispatched twice. Invariant~(II) governs the events
that \emph{reach} playback; it is not contradicted by \texttt{LeaseExpire}
discarding a still-buffered event, since a discarded click was buffered while
disconnected and never dispatched at all --- its loss is invariant~(III)'s
gap-boundedness, the deliberate stale-drop of fork~(B), not a double or missing
dispatch of a delivered event.

\subsection{Initialisation}

The daemon boots the listener with the socket down, no lease yet, and empty
histories.

\begin{schema}{LuxListenerInit}
  LuxListener'
  \where
  mode' = disconnected \\
  conns' = 0 \\
  subs' = 0 \\
  buffer' = \langle \rangle \\
  dispatched' = \langle \rangle \\
  fired' = \langle \rangle \\
  leasePhase' = noLease \\
  leaseAge' = 0 \\
  present' = zfalse
\end{schema}

\subsection{Connect --- open the one socket}

From \texttt{disconnected} with nothing buffered, \texttt{voxd} dials
\texttt{luxd} and authenticates. This is the first, clean connection --- the
buffered-reconnect path is \texttt{ReconnectAndDrain} below --- so
$buffer = \langle \rangle$ is a precondition. Authenticating is contact, so the
lease is born (or revived) into \texttt{held} at age zero.

\begin{schema}{Connect}
  \Delta LuxListener
  \where
  mode = disconnected \\
  buffer = \langle \rangle \\
  mode' = connected \\
  conns' = 1 \\
  subs' = 0 \\
  buffer' = buffer \\
  dispatched' = dispatched \\
  fired' = fired \\
  leasePhase' = held \\
  leaseAge' = 0 \\
  present' = ztrue
\end{schema}

\subsection{Subscribe --- establish the single subscription}

From \texttt{connected}, \texttt{voxd} subscribes to the music topics. Because a
\texttt{connected} state already has an empty buffer (invariant~(III)), the
subscription starts clean. Subscribing is contact and renews the lease.

\begin{schema}{Subscribe}
  \Delta LuxListener
  \where
  mode = connected \\
  mode' = subscribed \\
  conns' = 1 \\
  subs' = 1 \\
  buffer' = buffer \\
  dispatched' = dispatched \\
  fired' = fired \\
  leasePhase' = held \\
  leaseAge' = 0 \\
  present' = ztrue
\end{schema}

\subsection{ReceiveEvent --- dispatch one event, exactly once}

While subscribed, an inbound \texttt{ev?} is dispatched straight to playback:
\texttt{dispatched} grows by exactly that one event, and \texttt{fired} by the one
playback transition \texttt{dispatch~ev?}. The single append is invariant~(II);
the paired append preserves invariant~(V), since
$dispatched' \comp dispatch = (dispatched \comp dispatch) \cat \langle
dispatch~ev? \rangle = fired'$. Receiving is contact, so the lease is renewed.

\begin{schema}{ReceiveEvent}
  \Delta LuxListener \\
  ev? : MusicEvent
  \where
  mode = subscribed \\
  mode' = subscribed \\
  conns' = 1 \\
  subs' = 1 \\
  buffer' = buffer \\
  dispatched' = dispatched \cat \langle ev? \rangle \\
  fired' = fired \cat \langle dispatch~ev? \rangle \\
  leasePhase' = held \\
  leaseAge' = 0 \\
  present' = ztrue
\end{schema}

\subsection{Drop --- lose the socket, keep the lease}

The socket drops from \texttt{connected} or \texttt{subscribed}. The connection
counters fall to zero, but a drop is \emph{not} contact: the lease keeps ageing
in whatever phase it held, so the menu stays present in the grace window. The
buffer, empty at the moment of the drop, is now ready to accumulate.

\begin{schema}{Drop}
  \Delta LuxListener
  \where
  mode \neq disconnected \\
  mode' = disconnected \\
  conns' = 0 \\
  subs' = 0 \\
  buffer' = buffer \\
  dispatched' = dispatched \\
  fired' = fired \\
  leasePhase' = leasePhase \\
  leaseAge' = leaseAge \\
  present' = present
\end{schema}

\subsection{EnqueueDuringGap --- buffer a click Hub-side}

While the socket is down but the menu is still present (the lease
\texttt{held}), a user click publishes an event that the Hub buffers rather than
delivers. The event is appended to \texttt{buffer}; nothing is dispatched, and
the lease --- untouched by \texttt{voxd} --- keeps ageing. Once the lease expires
the menu withdraws, so this transition is disabled ($present = ztrue$ is
required), and no event can be buffered after withdrawal.

\begin{schema}{EnqueueDuringGap}
  \Delta LuxListener \\
  ev? : MusicEvent
  \where
  mode = disconnected \\
  present = ztrue \\
  mode' = mode \\
  conns' = conns \\
  subs' = subs \\
  buffer' = buffer \cat \langle ev? \rangle \\
  dispatched' = dispatched \\
  fired' = fired \\
  leasePhase' = leasePhase \\
  leaseAge' = leaseAge \\
  present' = present
\end{schema}

\subsection{ReconnectAndDrain --- automatic reconnect, drain in order}

The automatic reconnect re-establishes the socket and re-subscribes in one step,
and the Hub drains the whole buffer in arrival order. It is enabled only while
the lease is still \texttt{held} --- the grace window --- so it is precisely the
reconnect that \emph{wins} the race against the lease clock; a reconnect that
loses (the lease has expired) is a fresh registration, not a drain
(Section~\ref{sec:regfresh}). This is the heart of the gap-bounded
invariant~(III): \texttt{dispatched} gains exactly \texttt{buffer}, appended in
order, and \texttt{buffer} is emptied, so every buffered event that survived the
window is dispatched exactly once and none is lost or reordered. \texttt{fired}
gains the matching playback transitions, preserving invariant~(V) because
$dispatched' \comp dispatch = (dispatched \comp dispatch) \cat (buffer \comp
dispatch) = fired'$. Reconnecting is contact and renews the lease.

\begin{schema}{ReconnectAndDrain}
  \Delta LuxListener
  \where
  mode = disconnected \\
  leasePhase = held \\
  mode' = subscribed \\
  conns' = 1 \\
  subs' = 1 \\
  buffer' = \langle \rangle \\
  dispatched' = dispatched \cat buffer \\
  fired' = fired \cat (buffer \comp dispatch) \\
  leasePhase' = held \\
  leaseAge' = 0 \\
  present' = ztrue
\end{schema}

\subsection{LeaseRenew --- a heartbeat}

A subscribed but idle \texttt{voxd} keeps its lease alive by a heartbeat: any
authenticated contact with no other effect resets the age to zero. It is enabled
whenever \texttt{voxd} has a live socket ($mode \neq disconnected$); a
disconnected \texttt{voxd} cannot heartbeat and must reconnect instead. This is
the pure form of invariant~(IV)'s ``renewed on any contact''.

\begin{schema}{LeaseRenew}
  \Delta LuxListener
  \where
  mode \neq disconnected \\
  mode' = mode \\
  conns' = conns \\
  subs' = subs \\
  buffer' = buffer \\
  dispatched' = dispatched \\
  fired' = fired \\
  leasePhase' = held \\
  leaseAge' = 0 \\
  present' = ztrue
\end{schema}

\subsection{Tick --- time passes with no contact}

With no contact, a second elapses and the held lease ages by one, staying below
the cap. The final second before the cap is \texttt{LeaseExpire}, not
\texttt{Tick}, so the guard is $leaseAge < leaseTtl - 1$; this keeps
$leasePhase = held \implies leaseAge < leaseTtl$ true after the step. Nothing
else moves.

\begin{schema}{Tick}
  \Delta LuxListener
  \where
  leasePhase = held \\
  leaseAge < leaseTtl - 1 \\
  leaseAge' = leaseAge + 1 \\
  mode' = mode \\
  conns' = conns \\
  subs' = subs \\
  buffer' = buffer \\
  dispatched' = dispatched \\
  fired' = fired \\
  leasePhase' = leasePhase \\
  present' = present
\end{schema}

\subsection{LeaseExpire --- the menu withdraws}

When a held lease reaches its last second with still no contact, it expires: the
phase becomes \texttt{expired} at the full \texttt{leaseTtl}, the menu withdraws
($present' = zfalse$), and the Hub tears the socket down
($mode' = disconnected$) --- the modelled image of ``\texttt{voxd} died, so its
menu entries withdraw''. The buffer is \emph{cleared}
($buffer' = \langle \rangle$): this is fork~(B), settled with the lux agent.
\texttt{luxd}'s lease sweep drops the dead session together with its menu
callbacks and its Hub-side invocation hold in one motion --- and the hold is a
bounded per-session deque in any case (oldest-dropped past capacity), so an
expired session's buffered clicks are gone twice over. Any click undrained when
the lease lapses is discarded as stale; this is exactly the gap-boundedness of
invariant~(III), and it restores the strengthened state predicate
$buffer \neq \langle \rangle \implies present = ztrue$, which a preserved buffer
would have broken the instant the menu withdrew. Because the precondition is
$leaseAge = leaseTtl - 1$, expiry is reachable only after the full tick chain
from the last renewal --- invariant~(IV)'s ``only after \texttt{leaseTtl} with no
contact''.

\begin{schema}{LeaseExpire}
  \Delta LuxListener
  \where
  leasePhase = held \\
  leaseAge = leaseTtl - 1 \\
  mode' = disconnected \\
  conns' = 0 \\
  subs' = 0 \\
  buffer' = \langle \rangle \\
  dispatched' = dispatched \\
  fired' = fired \\
  leasePhase' = expired \\
  leaseAge' = leaseTtl \\
  present' = zfalse
\end{schema}

\subsection{Register-fresh: re-entry after a dead session}
\label{sec:regfresh}

Once the lease has expired, the session is gone Hub-side --- its menu entry
withdrawn, its buffer swept. \texttt{voxd} does not \emph{resume} the dead
session; it \emph{re-registers}. Because \texttt{voxd}'s lux identity is
deterministic, a re-registration re-identifies under the same name, re-registers
the ``Music'' menu entry, re-subscribes, and re-pushes its scene from
\texttt{ProgramService} state --- nothing depends on any surviving connection
state. In this model that re-entry is \texttt{Connect} (then \texttt{Subscribe}):
its precondition $buffer = \langle \rangle$ holds vacuously after an expiry (the
buffer was swept), so the re-entry starts a \emph{fresh} connection with an empty
buffer and a fresh lease, and there is no \texttt{ReconnectAndDrain} path out of
\texttt{expired} to resume a stale buffer. The register-fresh re-entry and the
first boot connection are therefore the same transition, exactly as a warm and a
cold start of the socket are indistinguishable once the buffer is empty.

That no click can arrive at a dead session is already a fact of the model:
\texttt{EnqueueDuringGap} requires $present = ztrue$, and expiry sets
$present' = zfalse$, so buffering is disabled the moment the menu withdraws.
Only clicks buffered \emph{before} expiry are ever at risk, and those are
precisely the ones the lease race decides.

\subsection{Why the split from PlayerView holds}

The separation of this machine from the \texttt{PlayerView} of
Sections~\ref{sec:state}--\ref{sec:conn} is the same one the design draws: a
transition's \emph{trigger} --- a lux click carried over this connection, or a
CLI call --- is immaterial to the view's invariants, so \texttt{PlayerView} needed
no connection state to be modelled. What this section adds is only the plumbing
that carries the trigger, and its own invariants (I--V) live entirely in the
connection state, disjoint from the view's I1--I3.
